Input Lag Lab

Hello. This project is dedicated to researching and eliminating the impact of the electrical component on input lag in competitive gaming.

What is Input Lag, really?

To put it simply: Input Lag is the time it takes for your system to turn a physical action into a visible result on the screen. It is often confused with network ping, but they are entirely different. Ping is the delay in communication with a server; input lag is the delay within your local workstation.

The chain where milliseconds are lost

Latency doesn't occur in just one place; it accumulates at every stage:

  • Peripherals: How fast the mouse controller processes the click/movement.
  • USB Bus & PC: The speed of signal transmission through the motherboard.
  • System (Windows): Time spent processing the frame in the render queue.
  • Monitor: How fast the matrix displays the updated pixels.

Why are we hunting this?

In competitive shooters like Valorant or CS, the battle is fought over milliseconds. High input lag creates a "floaty" sensation — as if your crosshair is attached to a rubber band and is always slightly lagging behind your hand.

When latency is minimized, you get a sense of direct contact: your aim becomes sharp, and shots register instantly.

Important: On this site, we look beyond Windows settings to fundamental issues: power grid quality, electromagnetic interference (EMI), and lack of grounding.
