The Upgrade Trap: MSI A750GL vs. Old Wiring

I previously used a budget Aerocool VP-650. With it, static was barely noticeable. Upgrading to a modern MSI MAG A750GL (ATX 3.0) was supposed to be a fix, but things paradoxically got worse.

"The problem isn't the PSU itself — the MSI unit filters noise perfectly. But all that 'filtered' electrical noise has nowhere to go in my 60-year-old aluminum wiring without grounding. The excess potential leaks into the PC case: my finger literally stings from the microphone, and mouse tracking turns into mud."

This proves: buying a high-end PSU without fixing the foundation (grounding) is like trying to run a supercar on low-octane fuel with a 180V outlet.

RAWM ER21PRO: Worst result. The sensor spun out randomly. I had to return it under warranty because playing was impossible. I strongly advise against it with poor electrical conditions.
Pulsar X2 Medium (Wired): Even on a wire, there is no sense of direct control. Likely, proper shielding was sacrificed for ultra-light weight.
Razer DeathAdder V2 (Wired): The only mouse that remains somewhat playable. Its older internals and cable seem better shielded than modern ultra-light models.

However, the "electrical swamp" effect is relentless. Over time, even this mouse starts to "leak." Static and port noise build up until the sensor feels heavy and hit registration degrades, though not as fast as other models.

Before replacing the wiring, the outlet was sitting at 180V. Once I finish the electrical panel overhaul using Hager components, I will post the updated results.
